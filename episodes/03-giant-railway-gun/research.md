# Giant Railway Gun: Why the Army Had to Bring the Railway

## Verified numerical claims and attribution

The German army asked Krupp to study a giant gun in 1935 for use against French fortifications. Production was too late for the 1940 campaign in France. The gun was later used at Sevastopol. The contemporary retrospective distinguishes the original task from its later use [1]. The museum association describes the 80 cm weapon and a mass exceeding 1,000 tonnes [2].

## Unverified — exclude from narration and artwork

Exact shot counts and effects attributed to particular shots.

## Scope and treatment

Discuss a procurement brief, an immovable calendar and the burden of hauling extraordinary mass. Explain opportunity cost using a freight manifest many ordinary items could have filled. Distinguish proof something could be built from proof it was a sensible use of a railway. Show crates and a stationary generic museum silhouette. No attack sequence, operation, dimensions for reproduction or aiming details. No Nazi insignia, no admiring combat spectacle.
